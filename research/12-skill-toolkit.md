# 12 — The skill toolkit: what the Outlaw Rogue kit teaches a five-button class

## The question

Why is the WoW Outlaw Rogue kit loved in Mythic+, and what of it survives a five-button, ten-key class (STATE.md D31)?

Matt, 2026-10-20, verbatim: "look at why people love playing outlaw rogue for a sec: you can pull threat, evasion,
cloak, flask, invis, re pull, grapple, cheap, ambush, grapple, sucker keeps pack alive if tank dies... there needs to
be this level of "skill" in the game... and playing into mechanics or builds like this will encourage the late game
way more than just hitting something lmao". That quote is the primary source for "loved"; no survey or designer
statement measuring Outlaw's popularity was found (UNCONFIRMED beyond Matt's account). Related ruling (D30 quote 3):
"Once threat is initialized, it cant be taken except for abilities... other characters need some toolkit to take
threat, or an upgraded ability".

## What players hate

1. **Button clutter and APM tax.** An Outlaw player on the official forums: "It's like needing the APM of a StarCraft
   pro just to beat the tank in damage" and "I feel like I'm playing whack-a-mole with my GCDs", while other classes
   "press a couple of buttons and blast past us". The toolkit is loved; the rotation load around it is not. Source:
   [Blizzard EU forums](https://eu.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/the-outlaw-rogue-experience-in-mythic-when-you-need-a-phd-in-apm-to-out-dps-a-tank/530195).
2. **Maintenance buffs.** The same post calls Slice and Dice upkeep "as underwhelming as it did when it was first
   introduced": a button pressed to keep a number up, not to answer anything. Source: same thread.
3. **Skip tools that stop working.** Icy Veins warns that many more enemies now detect through Stealth or
   Invisibility, so a routing tool's value depends on content outside the player's control (search-result excerpt,
   [Icy Veins](https://icy-veins.com/wow/outlaw-rogue-pve-dps-mythic-plus-tips)).

## Steal / adapt / avoid

**Steal** (`[unsourced opinion]` where it goes past the table):
1. **Multi-purpose buttons.** Vanish is a defensive, a threat reset, a root break and an opener enabler at once; one
   key answers several situations, so knowing which situation you are in is the skill.
2. **Outplay windows.** Evasion, Cloak and Feint are short and timed to a read of the incoming hit; a well-timed press
   turns a lethal mechanic into nothing, so the cast bar is the prompt and the response is the skill.
3. **Emergency saves.** A dying run has a way back that one player can execute (the kite, the Vanish at a wipe), which
   is test 3 of BIBLE section 0 ("no run ruined by one mistake") seen from the player's side.
4. **Reward for knowledge.** Which debuff Cloak removes, which mob to Sap, when Tricks starts a pull: the kit pays
   players who know the dungeon, which keeps high keys interesting after the rotation is learned.

**Adapt**:
- **Routing and skip tools.** Shroud and Sap change which pulls happen at all, so the route is part of the kit. BIBLE
  section 9 rules out invisibility and skip consumables, so in Keyrun the route lives in the walk-past-the-ring skip
  and tight movement (F), not in stealth.
- **Threat routing (Tricks).** Becomes generic take/give/drop threat ops any class can carry through a Facet or fork
  (D30 rule 3, D31 rule 3), never a class-owned special case.

**Avoid**:
- **The Outlaw button count.** The tools are the appeal; the 20-plus binds and maintenance buffs are the complaint
  above. Every tool must sit on a key the class already has (BIBLE section 2, D4).
- **A full threat reset inside a locked room.** D27 shuts the gate on pull start, so "drop" passes threat to the next
  holder rather than resetting the pack.

## Verdict

Keep five rotational buttons and ten keys. Get Outlaw-level depth by giving every class, through Facets and forks on
the keys it already has (R utility, E defensive, F movement and one rotational), a threat take, a reset or hand-off, a
kite tool and an emergency save, each pressed on the tick a cast bar or telegraph calls for. The toolkit is chosen at
build time and paid for in power, and it is executed under cast bars, so knowing the dungeon becomes the late-game skill.
