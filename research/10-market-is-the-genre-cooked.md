# Is the genre cooked?

**The question:** WoW-made-easy games are dying and players buy boosts instead of playing — is co-op dungeon
progression a dead genre before Keyrun goes down this rabbit hole, or is there real desire for a fun,
hard, class-deep co-op dungeon crawler?

## Death signals

Failures cluster around three stated causes, not "nobody wants co-op dungeons":

- **Fellowship** (closest analog to Keyrun's pitch) — 77% drop in its first month, a further 40.27% drop by Dec 3 2025, down to 216 concurrent at a January 2026 low. Players cite thin post-launch content, a fragmented multi-region/multi-league queue, and no streamer pipeline. [Steam discussion, "I don't think Fellowship is going to last"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2352620/discussions/0/694246561083948674/?ctp=3)
- **Helldivers 2** — creative director Johan Pilestedt admitted balance changes "gone too far"; 30-day Steam reviews went "Mostly Negative," community nickname "Nerfdivers." Cause: over-aggressive nerf/buff cadence killing the fun, not lack of demand for the genre. [techradar.com](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/consoles-pc/helldivers-2-director-agrees-that-balancing-has-gone-too-far)
- **Monster Hunter Wilds** — technical performance + a review-bombing campaign, not genre fatigue: still 4.3-4.5 star rated on console while "Overwhelmingly Negative" on Steam. [gamerant.com](https://gamerant.com/monster-hunter-wilds-player-count-decline-steam-users-report/)
- **Dauntless** — Phoenix Labs' Dec 2024 "Awakening" update slowed progression and locked weapons behind a new paid resource; game shut down entirely in 2025. Cause: monetization/progression redesign, not the co-op hunt loop. [80.lv postmortem](https://80.lv/articles/lessons-learned-from-the-rise-of-a-new-take-on-monster-hunter-and-its-sudden-collapse), [pcgamer.com layoffs](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/dauntless-developer-phoenix-labs-lays-off-employees-and-cancels-in-development-projects-says-its-the-last-resort-to-ensure-phoenix-labs-can-survive/)
- **Anthem** — BioWare/EA; misaligned single-player-studio-does-live-service vision, Frostbite dev hell, saturated looter-shooter market at launch; servers finally shut Jan 12 2026, seven years after release. Cause: production dysfunction, not core loop rejection. [thegamer.com](https://www.thegamer.com/anthem-servers-shutdown-bioware/)
- **Bleeding Edge** — Ninja Theory ended support after 10 months; launch month averaged only 480 concurrent, peak ever 1,464. Cause: hero-brawler genre was already saturated (LawBreakers, Battleborn, Crucible all failed the same year-class) — a genre-crowding failure, not a co-op-dungeon failure. [engadget.com](https://www.engadget.com/ninja-theory-bleeding-edge-development-ended-195426467.html)

## What the boosting economy says

WoW's Mythic+ population itself has been shrinking and Blizzard has repeatedly re-engineered the mode to fight it:

- Dragonflight S1 → S2: 3,886,920 → 3,002,158 active characters, **-22.77%**. DF S1 → TWW S1 (same point in season): **-17.19%** (3,886,920 → 3,218,740). [Icy Veins](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/news/player-activity-in-war-within-raids-and-mythic-back-to-the-old-pattern-but-more-stable/)
- TWW S1 → TWW S2: **-30.85%** at 19 days in, attributed to "bad experiences with delayed or missing tuning during S1" and a harsh standard-dungeon-to-M0 difficulty step. [Icy Veins](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/news/player-activity-in-war-within-raids-and-mythic-back-to-the-old-pattern-but-more-stable/)
- Raider.IO data cited by Icy Veins: participation fell **75% between Week 1 and Week 14 of TWW S1** versus the equivalent Dragonflight window. [Icy Veins "Mythic+ in Crisis"](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/news/mythic-in-crisis-blizzard-addresses-decline-with-major-changes-for-season-2/)
- Blizzard's response was structural, three separate times: (1) a full pre-TWW difficulty **renumbering** (old M15 → M5, Heroic → ~old M0, M0 → M10) explicitly "to serve the community who enjoys dungeon content but feels that Mythic+ content isn't for them due to the pressures of taking part"; (2) TWW S2 **removed keystone depletion** above a rating threshold and re-tuned dungeons for a smoother curve; (3) Midnight S3 (launching now, Sep 2026) makes the top 1%/5% achievements **spec-relative** instead of population-wide. All three are anti-gatekeeping, not difficulty-reduction, moves. [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/wow-dungeons-are-getting-a-difficulty-overhaul-and-its-more-than-a-little-confusing/1100-6521798/), [Icy Veins S2](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/news/mythic-in-crisis-blizzard-addresses-decline-with-major-changes-for-season-2/), [Blizzard Watch Midnight S3](https://blizzardwatch.com/2026/09/14/midnight-season-3-brings-new-mythic-dungeon-pool-shakes-high-end-season-rewards)
- The Mythic+ Rating/Score system, built to measure skill, was co-opted as a **PUG-leader screening gate**: "critics felt the introduction of Mythic+ rating would tempt players to unfairly screen players from unrelated content." Players who can, route around it by hosting their own key instead of applying — described as "free boosting." [dotesports headline via search](https://dotesports.com/wow/news/wow-player-finds-an-ingenious-way-to-bypass-gatekeeping-in-mythic-dungeons)
- Boosting itself: pricing is tiered like a labor market, not a flat pay-wall — roughly $5-15 for a timed +10, $20-45 for Keystone Master, $20-60 for Heroic raid, and "quote-based... into the hundreds" for Mythic progression/Gladiator/Rank 1, scaling ~3x per key-level step because "the skill requirement is high enough that the booster community sets rates around real-world labor costs, not gold-per-token math." [nerdly.co.uk](https://www.nerdly.co.uk/2026/03/13/wow-boost-price-guide-what-affects-the-cost-of-carries-raids-and-pvp-services/)
- Blizzard's ToS draws the line at *organized, real-money* carries — gold-for-gold help between players is allowed; an August 2026 ban wave targeted organized boosting communities, and Classic Anniversary realms now actively ban and enforce against GDKP/gold-selling. Two separate 2025-2026 enforcement pushes on two different product lines = a persistent, not one-off, problem. [wow-anniversary.fi](https://wow-anniversary.fi/en/uutiset/bluepostit/is-blizzard-banning-gold-boosting-rmt-gdkp-rules-2026/)
- Context floor: the general ELO/carry-boosting market across LoL/Overwatch/Dota was independently estimated at $170M (2019) sliding to $119M (2021) — RMT-adjacent carry demand is real money, industry-wide, not a WoW quirk. [Wikipedia: Boosting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boosting_(video_games))

**Synthesis** `[unsourced opinion, grounded in the above]`: the pricing structure (cheap at the entry tier, skill-priced at the top) plus the score-as-gate mechanic plus three rounds of Blizzard anti-gatekeeping patches together suggest people mostly pay to skip the **social screening and qualification grind** standing between them and a group — not to skip the boss fight itself. Buyers usually still want to *play* the loot they bought; they're buying past the PUG-leader's rating check, not past the dungeon.

## Whitespace

Fellowship (Chief Rebel, built by WoW/Diablo IV/Helldivers 2 veterans, EA Oct 2025) is the closest thing that
exists to Keyrun's exact pitch today: no quests, no leveling filler, pre-made 4-player trinity heroes (2 each
tank/heal/dps at EA), "Curses" that work like M+ Affixes on endlessly-scaling dungeons, and a stated
cosmetics-only monetization stance. PC Gamer's own framing: "WoW players should try Fellowship's dungeon-only
take on MMOs — it's like a fast and breezy version of WoW's party play." [pcgamer.com](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/wow-players-should-try-fellowships-dungeon-only-take-on-mmos-its-like-a-fast-and-breezy-version-of-wows-party-play/)

It proved the demand (Mostly Positive reviews, 480k units, 9M dungeons in month one) and then proved the
execution risk: population fragmented across regions and "Leagues" broke matchmaking at exactly the low-pop
tail Keyrun would also face, to the point the devs are still shipping "multi-League queueing" and an "Eternal
Queue" fix well into 2026 because "many players are searching for groups simultaneously but are effectively
missing one another." [all.gg Q&A recap](https://all.gg/news/matchmaking-leagues-tokens-big-fellowship-qa-recap/), [Icy Veins Eternal Queue](https://www.icy-veins.com/fellowship/news/fellowship-developer-update-big-fixes-and-future-changes/)

The nearest *retained* precedent isn't a WoW-clone at all — it's Darktide/Vermintide: real class/career depth,
queue-based matchmaking into genuinely hard content, sustained 3-7+ years on a free-core/paid-cosmetic-class
model. It lacks WoW's bounded "gear score ladder" progression, using talent trees and mission-difficulty
unlocks instead. Deep Rock Galactic is the nearest precedent for **surviving low population**: solo-with-bot
play (Bosco) keeps the loop alive even when the multiplayer queue is thin — direct validation of Keyrun's
already-locked `bots/` bots-first stack decision (CLAUDE.md D1).

No shipped, retained game combines all three of (a) queue-based matchmaking into (b) genuinely hard content
with (c) real class/trinity depth *and* (d) bounded, non-infinite progression, and stays alive past year one.
Fellowship is the live experiment answering exactly that question right now, and as of Sep 2026 it is failing
on population/live-ops execution, not on the core loop — its own review score and hour-count say the loop
itself is well liked. Whether that's a design gap (nobody has solved low-pop matchmaking + bounded progression
together) or a market gap (the addressable audience for "want the WoW dungeon feel, don't want the MMO around
it" is real but too small to sustain a live-ops cadence) is not yet resolved by the data.

## Verdict

Not cooked. The evidence: DRG (+20% 30-day growth, six years in), Darktide/Vermintide (3-7yr retention), and
Fellowship's own month-one numbers (480k units, 9M dungeons, Mostly Positive) all show real, current demand for
exactly this loop. What's cooked is the live-service playbook around it: infinite-cadence content, rating-gated
PUGs, and balance-patch whiplash killed Helldivers 2, Dauntless, and Fellowship's population, not the core fun.
Keyrun retains players under two conditions: (1) the queue must never die — bots-first solo/duo play (already
locked, D1) has to be a fully valid path at any population size, the way DRG's Bosco is and Fellowship's
region-split queue currently isn't; (2) progression must stay genuinely bounded and replayable without an
arms-race content cadence — a tight, finite build space (≤5 rotational abilities) that stays fun on repeat runs,
so retention doesn't depend on out-shipping Blizzard or Arrowhead.
