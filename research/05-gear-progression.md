# Gear Progression

## 1. The question

What gear/loot loop keeps people queueing for "one more run" without becoming a treadmill?

## 2. What the best games do

1. **WoW — Great Vault: a weekly deterministic choice, not a per-run roll.** Instead of loot dropping every run, M+/raid/PvP activity fills a 9-slot vault; once a week the player picks ONE item from a small, visible menu of options. Design lead Ion Hazzikostas has said the point is agency: taking a specific item from the Vault "is giving a player a sense of agency, providing them a reward for accomplishments over the prior week," and is "a more deterministic path towards loot that some higher-end players favor" than raw drop RNG. Source: [Warcraft Tavern, BlizzCon 2026 Ion & Holly interview](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/forever/news/ion-holly-interview-blizzcon-2026/). Why it works: it converts many small, swingy RNG events (each run's drop chance) into one weekly high-information decision, which reads as control rather than chance — and it gives a hard floor ("I will get *something* this week") that keeps the treadmill from feeling like pure noise.

2. **WoW — Mythic+ weekly reset as a cadence anchor.** The keystone system times a dungeon, upgrades or downgrades the key on completion, and resets weekly, so "one more run" has a natural stopping point (this week's key is done) instead of an infinite pull. Source: [Icy Veins, Great Vault Guide](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/great-vault-guide) (describes the M+/raid/PvP activity tracks that feed the weekly vault). Why it works: a bounded weekly container prevents the FOMO of an always-refreshing queue; the stopping point is legible.

3. **Diablo IV — variable affixes + Tempering turn "the chase item" into an optimization target, not a binary find.** A Unique or Mythic Unique dropping isn't "done," it's the start of rolling its affix ranges and Tempering slots toward a build-optimal version. Source: [MMOJUGG, Diablo 4 Loot Filter Guide](https://www.mmojugg.com/news/diablo-4-loot-filter-guide.html); [Icy Veins, Diablo 4 loot filter announcement](https://www.icy-veins.com/d4/news/goodbye-screen-clutter-diablo-4-is-getting-a-loot-filter/). Why it works: it stretches a single lucky drop into many subsequent decisions (reroll this affix? temper for X?), extending the value of RNG without requiring more RNG.

4. **Diablo IV — loot filters as clutter control, shipped because players demanded it.** As of the *Lord of Hatred* expansion, Diablo 4 added an in-game loot filter (hide/highlight by item type, affix, specific Unique) after years of community requests; it was described as the number-one requested feature. Source: [Icy Veins, "Say Hello to Diablo 4 Loot Filter"](https://www.icy-veins.com/d4/news/goodbye-screen-clutter-diablo-4-is-getting-a-loot-filter/); [Fextralife, Diablo 4 Loot Filters guide](https://diablo4.wiki.fextralife.com/Loot+Filters). Why it works: high drop volume only feels good if the player can suppress noise and surface signal — the filter is what makes "more loot" not equal "more tedium."

5. **Path of Exile — currency-as-crafting means every drop is a lever, not just a gamble.** PoE has almost no vendor-bought crafting; instead, currency items (found as drops) are applied directly to gear to reroll or add affixes, so "trash" drops still have value as crafting fuel. GGG has said crafting design leans on the interaction of craftable "meta mods" and basic currencies, trying to keep a luck factor while making the system legible. Source: [Grindout.com, PoE 2 Crafting Guide](https://grindout.com/poe-2/guides/crafting). Why it works: it removes the "dead drop" problem — even a bad item roll still feeds the crafting economy, so time spent playing is never wasted.

6. **Path of Exile — GGG has publicly conceded when the loot loop goes stale and patched it.** GGG acknowledged endgame content had become "predictable and tedious" and overhauled loot/currency drops in a subsequent patch. Source: [EZG, "PoE Patch 3.29 Loot Nerf Explained"](https://www.ezg.com/blog/poe-patch-3-29-loot-nerf-explained-really-reduce-drops-or-did-farming-strategies-change). Why it matters for Keyrun: even a best-in-class loot designer treats "is this loop still fun" as an ongoing, re-litigated question, not a one-time solved problem.

7. **Last Epoch — Forging Potential turns crafting into a finite, spendable resource with visible odds.** Every item has a hidden-but-inspectable "durability" for crafting: each forge action (add/upgrade an affix) consumes some of it, and better-rarity items drop with more Forging Potential, so rare/exalted bases are worth building around. Source: [Last Epoch Support, "What is Forging Potential?"](https://support.lastepoch.com/hc/en-us/articles/46361900702363-What-is-Forging-Potential); [Last Epoch Tools, Forging Potential guide](https://www.lastepochtools.com/guide/section/forging_potential). Why it works: it bounds RNG — crafting can fail, but it can't fail forever or infinitely, so a player always knows roughly how many shots they have left, which keeps the tension "spend wisely" rather than "gamble endlessly."

8. **Dark and Darker / Escape from Tarkov — extraction risk makes the run itself the reward, not just the loot table.** Both are built so that loot found in a run is only "real" once you extract; dying loses it, permanently (except insured/secured items in Tarkov). Source: [TheGamer, "Dark And Darker Is Escape From Tarkov But With Swords and Spells"](https://www.thegamer.com/dark-and-darker-escape-from-tarkov/); [TEDx Stanley Park, "Escape From Tarkov Psychology: Fear, Greed & Risk"](https://tedxstanleypark.com/the-psychology-of-tarkov-how-fear-greed-and-risk-shape-every-raid-you-play/). Why it works: loss aversion makes trivial actions (opening a door, crossing a hallway) carry real weight, which is a cheap, systemic way to generate tension without new content — the risk itself is the game feel.

9. **Monster Hunter — deterministic, transparent, part-based drop tables replace "the loot fairy."** Rewards come from four separate, disclosed pools — target rewards (quest completion), part breaks, wound destroys, and carves — each with published drop percentages visible in the in-game monster guide. Source: community compilation of Monster Hunter World/Rise/Wilds drop-pool structure, cross-referenced against [Game8, "How to Break Monster Parts"](https://game8.co/games/Monster-Hunter-Rise/archives/381635) [unsourced opinion: exact "4 pools" terminology paraphrased from aggregated wiki/community knowledge, not one single primary source]. Why it works: players can target-farm a specific material by choosing which body part to hit, converting "I need item X" from blind luck into a skill-adjacent decision (break the right part, hunt the right monster).

10. **Deep Rock Galactic — a fully separate cosmetic-overclock track keeps power progression flat past a point.** Weapon Overclocks are gameplay-altering mods with real stat/mechanic changes, but Deep Rock also has a parallel line of purely **cosmetic** Overclocks (reward tracks alternate between "Cosmetic Infused Matrix Core" and "Overclock Infused Matrix Core"), so players can keep unlocking visible, meaningful rewards after their build is functionally complete. Source: [DRG Wiki, "Overclocks"](https://deeprockgalactic.fandom.com/wiki/Overclocks); [zleague, "Do Players Choose Cosmetic Overclocks Over Weapons?"](https://www.zleague.gg/theportal/deep-rock-galactic-do-players-choose-cosmetic-overclocks-over-weapons/). Why it works: separating "look different" from "number go up" lets the reward loop keep running indefinitely without inflating power, which is exactly the treadmill-avoidance mechanism WoW lacks.

11. **Hades — meta-progression makes every failed run still pay out.** Supergiant deliberately built many parallel channels of permanent progression (Mirror of Night upgrades, Infernal Arm upgrades, unlocked Boons, an evolving hub narrative) specifically so no run ever feels wasted and death is a structural beat, not a punishment. Source: [Tom's Guide, "Hades exclusive: Supergiant explains why we love (and hate) roguelikes"](https://www.tomsguide.com/uk/features/hades-exclusive-interview-supergiant). Why it works: it decouples "did I clear the run" from "did I make progress," which is the core anti-treadmill trick — every attempt banks something durable.

## 3. What players hate

1. **The Great Vault reads as a weekly chore/obligation, not a treat.** A widely-read Blizzard forum thread titled "Thank you for playing at the Weekly Chore Great Vault Casino!" captures the sentiment that tracking multiple weekly activity requirements to fill vault slots feels like homework, and the payout is still a gamble across a small option set. Source: [Blizzard Forums, "Thank you for playing at the Weekly Chore Great Vault Casino!"](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/thank-you-for-playing-at-the-weekly-chore-great-vault-casino/1506749). It also produces addons like GreatVaultChecker/ChoreBoard whose entire purpose is coping with the obligation. Source: [CurseForge, GreatVaultChecker](https://www.curseforge.com/wow/addons/greatvaultchecker).

2. **Item-level inflation makes past effort worthless and raises the floor faster than the ceiling.** Community threads describe a compounding effect where "power starts to feel like it explodes later in an expansion" — the percentage increase per tier stays similar, but the absolute stat gain keeps growing, so gear from even a few months ago is discarded outright and old content stops mattering. Source: [Blizzard Forums, "The power creep needs work"](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/the-power-creep-needs-work/1459615); [Blizzard Forums, "Lets talk about power creep and gear inflation"](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/lets-talk-about-power-creep-and-gear-inflation/862780). This is the textbook treadmill: the number goes up, but relative power (and the feeling of having earned it) does not.

3. **Blizzard's own devs admit the Vault produces "feels bad" moments by design.** Players report seeing an item in the Vault that would be an immediate upgrade, but their optimal long-term choice is a *different* item — the correct play feels punishing in the moment. Source: [Warcraft Tavern, BlizzCon 2026 interview](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/forever/news/ion-holly-interview-blizzcon-2026/). This is a sunk-cost/choice-paralysis trap: presenting three options where only one is "right" turns a reward moment into a math problem.

4. **Extraction-shooter risk can tip from "exciting" to "not worth playing."** Escape from Tarkov's hardcore wipes have driven parts of the community into what PC Gamer called "open revolt," leaving players "penniless and bored to tears with the grind," and Blizzard-adjacent analysis notes some players simply stop once they've accumulated enough gear because late-wipe Tarkov "loses its appeal when players already have everything they want and don't fear death." Source: [PC Gamer, "Escape from Tarkov begins to quietly U-turn..."](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/fps/escape-from-tarkov-begins-to-quietly-u-turn-after-last-weeks-hardcore-wipe-left-players-penniless-and-bored-to-tears-with-the-grind/). The lesson: risk-of-loss is exciting only within a band — too punishing (early wipe, nothing to lose from) or too safe (late wipe, nothing to fear) both kill the loop.

5. **PoE's RNG can feel like it removes agency entirely.** Community commentary notes "there is no way to reliably target-farm a desired Synthesised base anymore. You have to rely on the law of large numbers and hope to hit the jackpot" — when a system removes target-farming, players feel like spectators to a slot machine rather than participants making decisions. Source: [Grindout.com, PoE 2 Crafting Guide](https://grindout.com/poe-2/guides/crafting).

6. **Cosmetic-only rewards can feel like a bait-and-switch if marketed as gear.** Steam discussion titled "Cosmetic Overclocks Suck" shows some Deep Rock players resent finding a "cosmetic overclock" in a reward slot they expected to contain a power upgrade. Source: [Steam Community, "Cosmetic Overclocks Suck"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/548430/discussions/1/3826424631147231067/). Lesson for Keyrun: cosmetic and power rewards must be clearly labeled/separated in the UI before the player opens them, not revealed as a twist.

## 4. Steal / adapt / avoid

**Steal**
- The Great Vault's *weekly deterministic choice from a small, visible menu* — converts many small RNG events into one legible high-agency decision. Fits a ≤5-ability, few-slot kit perfectly: a weekly vault of 3 options is easy to read at a glance.
- Diablo IV's *build-changing affix, not just bigger numbers* — a chase item should unlock or reshape a playstyle (an ability behaves differently, a synergy hook activates) rather than simply raise a stat. This maps directly onto Keyrun's 5-ability/synergy-first constraint.
- Monster Hunter's *transparent, target-farmable drop source* — tell the player which run/boss mechanic drops which gear, with a visible rate, so farming feels like a skill decision ("beat the boss's enrage phase for the drop") not a slot pull.
- Deep Rock's *cosmetic track separated from power track* — lets "one more run" keep paying out after the build is functionally done, without inflating numbers. Directly solves the item-level-inflation problem at the design root.
- Hades' *meta-progression that banks something on every run, win or lose* — a failed dungeon run should still grant partial account-level progress (a currency, a small permanent unlock), so "one more run" isn't purely a re-roll of the same odds.

**Adapt**
- Last Epoch's *finite, visible crafting resource* — instead of infinite reroll RNG, give each gear piece a small number of "modification charges" so players make deliberate choices instead of grinding a slot machine; scale this down hard (Keyrun has few slots, so even 1–2 charges per item is meaningful).
- Extraction-game *risk of loss*, but bounded and opt-in — since Keyrun is a co-op dungeon (not PvP looter), full loss-on-death is likely too punishing (see "what players hate" #4); adapt the *tension*, not the *stakes*: e.g., a timed-run bonus-loot mechanic that is lost only if the party wipes or misses the timer, never a full gear-loss system.
- PoE's *currency-as-crafting-fuel* — even "bad" drops should feed something (disenchant into a small currency spent on the finite crafting charges above), so no drop is a dead end.

**Avoid**
- WoW's *item-level inflation treadmill* — do not let numeric power climb every content patch; if new gear is added, it should be sidegrade/playstyle-changing, not a strict upgrade that obsoletes the last cycle's chase items.
- The Vault's *"feels bad" trap-choice design* — never present a weekly reward menu where the mathematically correct pick visibly looks worse than a wrong one; if there's a hidden-value pick, surface the value (show projected stats/effects), don't hide it behind theorycrafting.
- PoE's *un-target-farmable currency* — avoid drop systems where players cannot point at a specific source and say "that's where I get the thing I need"; with only a few gear slots, every slot needs a legible source.
- Tarkov's *late-cycle apathy* — avoid a system where, once a player has "everything," death carries no weight and the loop goes slack; the cosmetic/meta-progression track (see Steal) is what keeps late-game engagement without re-inflating power.

## 5. Verdict

Gear should change *what the 5 abilities do* or *which synergy hooks light up*, never just their numbers — item-level inflation (WoW) is the treadmill; build-changing affixes (Diablo IV legendary powers) are the loop. Pair a weekly deterministic Vault-style choice (3 legible options, not a raw per-run roll) with Monster Hunter–style transparent, target-farmable drop sources tied to specific boss mechanics, so players can point at what they're farming. Bound crafting with a small, visible number of modification charges per item (Last Epoch's Forging Potential, scaled down) instead of infinite reroll RNG. Keep power and cosmetics on separate tracks (Deep Rock) so "one more run" keeps paying out post-cap without re-inflating numbers, and bank a small permanent account-level unlock on every run regardless of outcome (Hades) so no run is wasted. Do not add character-death gear loss (Tarkov/Dark and Darker) — Keyrun is co-op PvE with few slots; that stakes model fits a PvP looter, not a 5-ability dungeon crawler, and risks the late-cycle apathy Tarkov shows once players stop fearing loss.
